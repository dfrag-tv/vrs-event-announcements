- Australia
- New Zealand

The residency eligibility requirements published in the tournament Additional Information apply equally to all participants.

Connecting to Sydney servers from another country does not satisfy residency eligibility.

No additional unpublished citizenship, residency or regional eligibility filter may be introduced after publication except where required by applicable law or a mandatory platform requirement applying equally to all participants.

A participant is ineligible where an applicable active ban or restriction exists, including:

- ESL bans that prohibit tournament participation.
- eXTREMESLAND bans.
- Recognized match-fixing or competitive-integrity bans.
- Other applicable restrictions issued by a recognized tournament organizer, platform or esports authority.

Discovery of an applicable ban after registration may result in removal of the player or team, forfeiture of results, loss of prize money or loss of advancement.

# 3. Team and Roster Rules

## 3.1 Team Composition

Each registered roster must contain:

- Five starting players.
- Up to two substitute players.
- One coach.
- One nominated captain or authorized team representative, who may also be one of the registered players or the coach.

All members must satisfy the eligibility requirements in section 2.

Only properly registered and eligible participants may compete.

### 3.1.1 In-Game Names (Alias)

Participants may not use any in-game name (Alias) which:

- Contains vulgar or offensive language.
- Contains a sponsor or product name/short name.
- Is already in use by another participant.

Team names and other tournament-facing branding remain subject to **12.3**.

## 3.2 Multiple Teams

A player may represent only one team in the Oceania Local Qualifier.

Players must not:

- Register for multiple teams.
- Use another player's account.
- Allow another person to use their account.
- Compete under false identity information.
- Act as an undisclosed substitute.

## 3.3 Roster Continuity

A team must field at least three Core Members for the entirety of each official match.

Core Members are identified as follows:

- **Open Qualifier:** Core Members are the five players initially registered.
- **Elimination Stage and Playoffs:** Core Members are the five players who played the most maps in the previous stage.

A match forfeit will be declared where a roster does not field at least three Core Members for the entirety of an official match.

The team advancing to Shanghai must also satisfy any separately published central eXTREMESLAND roster-continuity requirement.

## 3.4 Substitutes

Only listed substitute players may be used.

A substitute must be named on the roster at least **24 hours before match start**.

A substitute may enter a map only **before the first round of record** of that map.

After roster lock on **Thursday, 24 September 2026**:

- no substitute may be added;
- no substitute may be removed;
- no substitute may be replaced by another player.

A locked listed substitute may still be selected into a match lineup. That selection is not a roster change.

Coaches are not substitutes except as provided in **3.5**.

## 3.5 Coaches

**Open Qualifier:** coaches are not permitted in the server.

**Elimination Stage and Playoffs:** a coach named before roster lock may occupy the FACEIT coach slot.

After roster lock, a coach cannot be added, removed or replaced.

A coach may be used as a playing substitute only in a documented emergency. Documented emergencies are limited to health issues or a verified power or connectivity failure. That use must not cause the team to field fewer than three Core Members.

Coaches must not discuss tactics during technical pauses.

## 3.6 Roster Lock

The final roster deadline is:

**Thursday, 24 September 2026**

After roster lock, roster changes are not allowed.

This includes adding, removing or replacing starting players, substitutes or coaches.

## 3.7 Extrajudicial Agreements

Gentlemen's agreements are not sanctioned or enforceable by the Tournament Organizer.

## 3.8 Team Transfer

A player may transfer to another team only if:

- the player has not played an official match for the original team; and
- the receiving roster is still unlocked.

After roster lock, no transfers are permitted.

The one-team rule in **3.2** continues to apply.

# 4. Tournament Structure

## 4.1 Stage 1 — Open Qualifier

The Open Qualifier is an official stage of the Oceania Local Qualifier. It is covered by this Ruleset and by the published Additional Information.

The Open Qualifier is not a ranked VRS stage. Results from the Open Qualifier are not a ranked VRS stage outcome. Global VRS is used only to seed the Open Qualifier as published in this Ruleset.

The Open Qualifier will be played online through FACEIT, starting:

**Saturday, 19 September 2026, 10:00 AEST (UTC+10)**

and continuing on:

**Sunday, 20 September 2026**

The Open Qualifier will use a:

**Single-elimination bracket**

The final bracket size and number of rounds will be determined after registration closes on **18 September 2026, 23:55 AEST (UTC+10)**.

The following format applies:

- Early rounds are Best-of-One.
- Matches directly determining qualification into the Stage 2 Elimination Stage are Best-of-Three.
- Eight teams advance to the Elimination Stage.

The exact round at which qualification matches begin will be published with the final bracket.

The Open Qualifier will be seeded using:

1. Global VRS-ranked rosters, highest to lowest;
2. unranked rosters ordered by FACEIT Elo;
3. FACEIT's deterministic process or a recorded random draw where required.

A roster without a Global VRS rank cannot be seeded above a VRS-ranked roster.

## 4.2 Stage 2 — Elimination Stage

Stage 2 Elimination Stage is a ranked VRS stage.

The eight qualified teams will progress into two four-team groups:

- Group A — four teams.
- Group B — four teams.

Each group will use a **four-team double-elimination / GSL-style format**.

All Elimination Stage matches are **Best-of-Three (Bo3)**.

The Top 2 teams from each group advance to the Playoffs.

Group-stage competition will take place from:

**30 September–2 October 2026**

## 4.3 Group Seeding

The eight qualified teams will be seeded using the **September 2026 official VRS** publication named in the Additional Information.

Global VRS-ranked teams will be ordered from highest to lowest Global VRS ranking, followed by unranked teams.

A roster without a Global VRS rank cannot be seeded above a VRS-ranked roster.

The intended group distribution is:

**Group A:** Seeds 1, 4, 5, 8
**Group B:** Seeds 2, 3, 6, 7

Where no participating roster has a Global VRS ranking, tournament administration will conduct and record a random draw.

## 4.4 Stage 3 — Playoffs

Stage 3 Playoffs is a ranked VRS stage.

The Top 2 teams from each group advance to a four-team double-elimination playoff bracket.

Initial playoff pairings will cross-seed the groups:

- Group A #1 vs Group B #2.
- Group B #1 vs Group A #2.

All playoff series are **Best-of-Three (Bo3)**.

The playoff bracket will contain:

1. Upper Semifinal 1.
2. Upper Semifinal 2.
3. Lower Round 1.
4. Upper Final.
5. Lower Final / Consolidation Final.
6. Grand Final.

There will be **no bracket reset** in the Grand Final.

## 4.5 Advancement

One team advances from the Oceania Local Qualifier to the designated Shanghai stage of eXTREMESLAND CS Asia Open 2026.

## 4.6 Vacancies and Replacements

Where a qualified team withdraws, is declared ineligible or is disqualified before entering the Elimination Stage, the vacant position will be offered using the following objective order:

1. Highest finishing non-qualified Open Qualifier team.
2. Highest Open Qualifier stage reached.
3. Match differential.
4. Map differential.
5. Round differential.
6. Head-to-head result, where applicable.
7. Initial published Open Qualifier seed.
8. Recorded random draw.

No discretionary direct invitation may be used to fill a vacancy.

Where a vacancy occurs after Elimination Stage competition begins, the published bracket and forfeiture rules apply unless an objective replacement procedure was published before tournament play began.

# 8. Map Pool and Veto

## 8.1 Official Map Pool

The official map pool is:

2. Mirage
3. Inferno
4. Cache
5. Ancient
6. Nuke
7. Anubis

If Valve or eXTREMESLAND changes the applicable competitive map pool before the Oceania Local Qualifier begins, an updated pool may be published.

The map pool will not ordinarily change after tournament play begins.

## 8.2 Veto Platform

Map vetoes will be conducted through the FACEIT match room or another tournament-administered FACEIT-supported process.

Only the registered captain or authorized team representative may make veto selections.

## 8.3 Best-of-One Veto

For a Bo1:

1. Team A bans one map.
2. Team B bans one map.
3. Team A bans one map.
4. Team B bans one map.
5. Team A bans one map.
6. Team B bans one map.
7. The remaining map is played.

A knife round determines the starting side unless FACEIT configuration requires otherwise.

## 8.4 Best-of-Three Veto

For a Bo3:

1. Team A bans one map.
2. Team B bans one map.
3. Team A selects one map.
4. Team B selects one map.
5. Team A bans one map.
6. Team B bans one map.
7. The remaining map is the deciding map.

For each team's selected map, the opposing team receives starting-side selection where supported.

The deciding map uses a knife round for starting-side selection unless otherwise configured by FACEIT.

## 8.5 Failure to Complete Veto

Failure to submit a veto within the required FACEIT voting period may result in:

- An automated FACEIT veto decision.
- Loss of the relevant veto choice.
- Tournament administrator intervention.
- Another appropriate competitive penalty.

# 10. Pauses, Interruptions and Restarts

## 10.1 Tactical Timeouts

Each team may use:

- Three 30-second tactical timeouts during regulation.
- One additional 30-second tactical timeout during overtime where configured.

Tactical timeouts must use the FACEIT or in-game timeout function.

## 10.2 Technical Pauses

A technical pause must be requested as soon as a technical problem becomes known.

Players must not discuss tactics during a technical pause.

Tournament administration may extend or modify technical-pause limits where necessary to resolve a legitimate issue.

## 10.3 Administrator Pauses

Tournament administrators may pause a match for:

- Server or FACEIT failure.
- Anti-cheat review.
- Player technical failure.
- Broadcast or production failure.
- Suspected rule violation.
- Competitive-integrity issue.
- Other material operational circumstances.

## 10.4 Player Disconnection

Where a player disconnects:

1. The affected team should request a technical pause where possible.
2. Tournament administration should be notified where the issue materially affects play.
3. The player must attempt to reconnect.
4. Play may resume in accordance with FACEIT functionality or administrator instruction.

## 10.5 Round Restoration

Tournament administration may restore a match from a valid server backup where supported.

In determining whether restoration is appropriate, administrators may consider:

- Damage dealt.
- Information gained.
- Utility used.
- Bomb status.
- Time elapsed.
- Other material competitive events.

## 10.6 Participant-Caused Errors

Rounds will not normally be restarted because of:

- Accidental purchases.
- Incorrect weapon drops.
- Mistimed utility.
- Player input errors.
- Failure to follow a known command or procedure.

## 10.7 Server Failure

Where a FACEIT server becomes unusable, tournament administration may:

- Restore a valid backup.
- Restart the affected round.
- Restart the map.
- Move the match to another authorized server.
- Reschedule the match.

## 10.8 Rematch

A full rematch may be ordered only where no fair and reliable alternative exists.

Tournament administration will determine the applicable map, score state, veto status, lineup and rescheduled time.

# 11. Anti-Cheat and Competitive Integrity

## 11.1 Prohibited Conduct

The following is prohibited:

- Cheating.
- Match fixing.
- Deliberate underperformance.
- Collusion.
- Bribery.
- Unauthorized wagering-related conduct.
- Account sharing.
- Player impersonation.
- Stream sniping.
- Unauthorized access to opponent information.
- Anti-cheat tampering.
- Falsification of tournament evidence.
- Deliberate exploitation of material game bugs.

## 11.2 Unauthorized Software

Players must not use any software, script, macro, configuration or hardware feature that creates an unauthorized competitive advantage.

This includes, without limitation:

- Aimhacks.
- Wallhacks.
- Radar hacks.
- Recoil-control scripts.
- Rapid-fire scripts.
- Automated movement scripts.
- Unauthorized macros.
- External information overlays.
- Visibility modifications.
- Unauthorized radar or texture modifications.
- Modified drivers used to obtain a competitive advantage.

## 11.3 Bugs and Exploits

Players must not deliberately exploit unintended game behavior.

Examples include:

- Entering inaccessible geometry.
- Viewing through map geometry in an unintended manner.
- Unreachable or materially bugged bomb plants.
- Unintended boosts creating prohibited positions or sightlines.
- Continuing to use an exploit after being instructed to stop.

Normal boosting and player stacking is permitted unless it produces an unintended exploit.

## 11.4 Match Manipulation

Participants must not:

- Predetermine a result.
- Intentionally lose.
- Manipulate map, round or score differentials.
- Exchange compensation for competitive conduct.
- Coordinate wagering.
- Provide inside information for wagering.
